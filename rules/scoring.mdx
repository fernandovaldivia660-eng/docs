Every objective in El Piso es Lava has its own win condition, so knowing what you're actually playing for changes your strategy entirely. Some rounds reward the fastest player, others reward the last one standing. This page breaks down exactly how the referee decides who wins — round by round and across a full multi-round session.

## Win conditions by objective

The objective for a round is set on the setup screen before play begins. The table below shows what each one requires to win.

| Objective | Win condition |
|---|---|
| **Copa** | First player to grab the cup wins immediately |
| **Vuelta** (Ida y vuelta) | First player to grab the cup **and** return it to base wins immediately |
| **Cacería** | Player with the most items fetched when time runs out |
| **Papa** (Pasa la copa) | Last player still standing (not eliminated) |
| **Estatuas** | Last player still standing |
| **Cadena** | Last player still standing |
| **Supervivencia** | Last player still standing |

<Info>
  For **Copa** and **Vuelta** (Ida y vuelta), the round ends the moment the win condition is met — the timer stops and the result screen appears right away, regardless of how much time was left.
</Info>

## When time runs out without a winner

For objectives where no one has clinched it by the buzzer, the referee uses a fallback rule.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Survival objectives" icon="heart">
    In **Papa** (Pasa la copa), **Estatuas**, **Cadena**, and **Supervivencia**, the player with the **most lives remaining** when the clock hits zero wins the round.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Cacería" icon="bag-shopping">
    In **Cacería**, the player who fetched the **most items** during the round wins. Lives are not a tiebreaker here.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Tiebreaks

If two or more players are tied on the deciding metric when the round ends (equal lives, equal items, or simultaneous cup grab), the referee calls it a **draw**. No partial points are awarded — all tied players share the result equally.

<Note>
  A draw means nobody's win counter increases for that round. Plan accordingly if you're behind on the scoreboard and need a clean victory.
</Note>

## Multi-round sessions

A full session can span as many rounds as you like. Round wins accumulate in the **scoreboard**, which is visible on the result screen after each round.

The scoreboard tracks three columns per player:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Wins** | Total rounds won across the session |
| **Items** | Total items fetched across all Cacería rounds |
| **Steps** | Total footsteps detected by the camera across all rounds |

<Info>
  The step counter uses the camera to detect movement. It's a fun stat rather than a scoring metric — no objective awards points purely for taking the most steps.
</Info>

## Final standings

When you're done playing, tap **Terminar partida** on the result screen. The referee calculates the overall session standings based on accumulated wins and announces the **overall champion** by voice. The final scoreboard is displayed so every player can see the full breakdown.

<Tip>
  If you want a meaningful final champion, agree on a fixed number of rounds before you start. For example, "first to 3 round wins" gives everyone a clear target and keeps the session from going on indefinitely.
</Tip>

## Starting a new round

When you tap **Siguiente ronda**, the round resets: lives and items return to their starting values, but **win counts carry over**. Every round is a fresh fight — but the overall session lead follows you in.
