When camera tracking is enabled, El Piso es Lava assigns each player a distinct color. The camera watches for that color moving across the floor grid and uses it to detect lava violations automatically. Choosing the right item to represent your color — and wearing it in the right way — makes the difference between smooth, hands-free refereeing and a dot that keeps blinking out.

## The eight player colors

Each color has a vivid, highly saturated hue so that it stands out against typical room backgrounds and clothing.

| Color | Hex | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rosa | `#FF2D6F` | Works well against light floors and cool-toned clothing |
| Lima | `#9BFF2D` | Stands out against dark floors and furniture |
| Cian | `#2DE5FF` | Great contrast against wood floors and brown sofas |
| Naranja | `#FF8A1F` | Avoid if your sofa or rug is orange or red |
| Morado | `#B14DFF` | Unusual hue — rarely conflicts with room decor |
| Amarillo | `#FFE12D` | High visibility; avoid cream or beige floors |
| Verde | `#12D46B` | Avoid if you have a lot of house plants in frame |
| Azul | `#2D6BFF` | Avoid blue jeans or blue upholstery in the play area |

<Note>
Your color is assigned by the app when you join the game. You cannot change it, but you can check your color on your player chip at any time.
</Note>

## What to wear or hold

The tracker needs to see a solid patch of your assigned color from above. Small or patterned items are harder to detect reliably. Here are the best options, roughly in order of how well they work:

- **Strip of colored paper or cardstock** — cheap, easy to cut, works with any printer. Tape it to your shoulder, hat brim, or the back of your hand.
- **Colored wristband or bracelet** — hold your wrist up or keep it on the side facing the camera.
- **Solid-color sock** — pulled up to the ankle and visible from above.
- **Sticky note on your shirt shoulder** — the bright adhesive paper works well if the color matches closely enough.
- **Small fabric square or felt piece** — cut a 10 × 10 cm square and pin it to your collar or hat.

<Tip>
Attach your color item to the **top of your body** — a hat, shoulder, or outstretched hand. The camera looks down from above, so an item on your wrist hanging at your side may disappear when you raise your arm or turn around.
</Tip>

## Saturation and brightness requirements

The tracker evaluates each sampled color by its hue, saturation, and brightness. To register during calibration and be detected during play, your color item must meet these minimums:

- **Saturation ≥ 0.32** during calibration — pastel or washed-out shades will be rejected at the registration step.
- **Saturation ≥ 0.30** during play — the live tracker uses a slightly relaxed threshold once you are in the game.
- **Brightness ≥ 0.20** — very dark items (even if technically the right hue) will be rejected.

If you tap your item during calibration and the app warns you that the color is too muted, try one of these fixes:

1. Move to a brighter part of the room and tap again.
2. Swap for a fresh, unbleached piece of paper in the correct color.
3. Hold the item flat and perpendicular to the camera so it does not cast a shadow on itself.

<Warning>
**Faded pastels will not work.** A light-pink wristband for Rosa, a pale-yellow sticky note for Amarillo, or a washed-out cyan shirt will all fail the saturation check. Use new, brightly colored paper or purpose-made items.
</Warning>

## Avoiding background conflicts

The tracker distinguishes player colors from each other and from the room background, but it can be confused if a background object shares the same hue as a player's color.

- **Scan the room before the game.** Look for sofas, rugs, walls, or large objects in any of the eight player colors.
- If a color in the room closely matches an assigned player color, ask the app host to manually reassign that player to a less conflicting color (if the game mode allows), or rearrange the camera angle to exclude the conflicting object.
- A few common conflicts to watch for:
  - **Naranja / Amarillo** — orange or yellow wood flooring, terracotta tiles.
  - **Verde / Lima** — houseplants, green rugs, grass visible through a window.
  - **Azul** — blue denim on non-playing bystanders, blue upholstery.

## Fixing a lost player

If a player's dot blinks, disappears, or their chip shows a **dim bead** during play, the tracker has lost their signal. The PiP overlay label will show **"N sin señal"** in red.

Try these steps in order:

1. **Check the item** — is it still visible from above? Reposition it on the shoulder or hat.
2. **Check the lighting** — move a lamp closer or open a blind to add more light to the play area.
3. **Re-run calibration** — open settings and restart the calibration wizard to re-sample the player's color under the current lighting.
4. **Switch color items** — replace the faded or crumpled item with a fresh piece of brightly colored paper.
5. **Change the color** — if the room background keeps interfering, a different player color may track more reliably in your specific space.

<Info>
Manual mode is always available as a fallback. If tracking problems persist, any player or the referee can tap a chip to deduct a life by hand — you do not need to stop the game.
</Info>
