El Piso es Lava can use your device's camera to referee the game without anyone lifting a finger. When camera tracking is on, the app watches the play area in real time, spots when a player steps on unmarked floor (lava), and even notices when someone grabs the cup — all through simple color-blob detection that runs entirely on your device.

## What camera tracking does

Camera tracking handles three jobs automatically:

- **Lava violations** — If your assigned color appears on an unmarked floor cell for 0.45 seconds, you lose a life. A 2.5-second cooldown prevents the same step from being counted twice.
- **Safe zone awareness** — Cells you mark as safe (sofas, cushions, chairs) are never flagged, so you can safely stand on furniture.
- **Cup grabs** — When the cup moves more than 11 % of the frame width away from its starting position, the app fires the *grabbed* event automatically.

<Note>
All processing happens locally in your browser. No video is sent to any server, and no machine-learning model is involved — only fast, lightweight color-blob detection.
</Note>

## What you need

To use camera tracking you need:

- A **modern browser** (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge) with camera permission granted.
- The page served over **HTTPS** or opened directly as a local file — browsers block camera access on plain HTTP.
- A **rear-facing camera** is preferred for a top-down view of the room (you can switch to front camera during calibration).
- **Good lighting** — bright, even light lets the tracker reliably find each player's color.
- **Vibrant, solid-color items** for each player to wear or hold (bracelet, sock, paper strip).

<Warning>
If you open El Piso es Lava inside an **iframe** — for example embedded in another site — the browser will block camera access regardless of permissions. Open the app directly in its own tab.
</Warning>

## The Picture-in-Picture overlay

While the game is running, a small Picture-in-Picture (PiP) overlay shows the live camera feed so you can keep an eye on tracking without leaving the main game screen. Inside the overlay:

- A **colored dot** appears for each player at their detected position.
- A **gold circle** marks the cup's tracked position.
- The label **"Rastreando"** confirms that tracking is working correctly.
- If a player goes undetected, their chip on the main screen shows a **dim bead**, and the PiP label switches to **"N sin señal"** in red (where *N* is the number of lost players).

If you see the "sin señal" warning, ask the affected player to hold their color item more visibly or move to a better-lit area.

## Camera vs. manual mode

Camera tracking is entirely optional. A manual tap is always available as a fallback — any player or the referee can tap a player's chip to subtract a life by hand.

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Well-lit room, players have vivid color items | Use camera tracking for hands-free refereeing |
| Dim room or no vibrant colors available | Use manual mode and tap chips to deduct lives |
| Embedded in an iframe or on plain HTTP | Camera unavailable — use manual mode |
| Quick game with young children | Manual mode is simpler to set up |
