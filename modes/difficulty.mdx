---

The difficulty pace controls the heartbeat of every round: how long you get to breathe during safe phases, how long you must survive during lava phases, and whether the game plays fair or messes with your head. Choosing the right pace can make the difference between a fun family afternoon and a genuinely frantic competition.

## At a glance

| Pace | Spanish name | Safe phase | Lava phase | Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Siesta | Siesta | 13–20 s | 3–5 s | Ideal for young children |
| Easy | Fácil | 9–15 s | 5–8 s | Relaxed and forgiving |
| Normal | Normal | 5–11 s | 6–11 s | Balanced for most groups |
| Metronome | Metrónomo | exactly 7 s | exactly 7 s | Perfectly predictable rhythm |
| Volcano | Volcán | 3–7 s | 9–14 s | Long lava, short rest |
| Tide | Marea | 13 s → 2.5 s | 4 s → 14 s | Escalates over the round |
| Betrayal | Traición | 4–9 s | 6–12 s | 35% fake lava, 30% sneak lava |
| Chaos | Caos | 2.5–6 s | 8–15 s | No mercy |

---

## Siesta

**Spanish name:** Siesta

Siesta is the gentlest pace available. You get generous safe windows of 13 to 20 seconds and very short lava phases of only 3 to 5 seconds. There is plenty of time to get everyone onto furniture and no real danger of being caught out.

<Note>
  Siesta is designed specifically for young children or anyone playing El Piso es Lava for the very first time. If you have kids under six at the party, start here.
</Note>

**What to expect:** Long, relaxed breaks with brief lava interruptions. You will almost never feel rushed.

---

## Fácil

**Spanish name:** Fácil

Fácil ("Easy") trims the safe window slightly to 9–15 seconds and extends lava phases to 5–8 seconds — still very manageable but with just enough tension to feel like a game. It is the natural next step after Siesta.

**What to expect:** A comfortable game where newer or less mobile players can compete without feeling overwhelmed. Mistakes happen occasionally, but there is always time to recover.

---

## Normal

**Spanish name:** Normal

Normal is the default and the best starting point for a mixed group of adults. Safe phases run 5–11 seconds and lava phases 6–11 seconds — roughly balanced, with enough randomness to keep everyone on their toes.

**What to expect:** Rounds feel brisk. You cannot afford to wander far from your nearest elevated surface, but there is still room for strategy and movement between lava phases.

---

## Metrónomo

**Spanish name:** Metrónomo

Metrónomo removes all randomness. Every safe phase lasts exactly 7 seconds. Every lava phase lasts exactly 7 seconds. The game ticks like a clock.

<Info>
  The predictable beat of Metrónomo sounds easier, but it is surprisingly difficult to play well. Once players learn the rhythm, the pressure to move efficiently in exactly 7 seconds becomes intense — especially in competitive Cacería or Ida y Vuelta games.
</Info>

**What to expect:** A mechanical, tension-ratcheting pace. Great for players who want to strategise and for spectators who can count along.

---

## Volcán

**Spanish name:** Volcán

Volcán ("Volcano") flips the balance: safe phases are cut to just 3–7 seconds while lava phases stretch out to a punishing 9–14 seconds. You scramble for safety and then wait a long time before you can move again.

**What to expect:** Highly physical rounds where your choice of elevated position is critical. Standing on an unstable cushion for 14 seconds is a real test of balance.

---

## Marea

**Spanish name:** Marea

Marea ("Tide") is unlike any other pace — it escalates over the course of a round. Safe phases begin long (around 13 seconds) and gradually shrink to as little as 2.5 seconds. Meanwhile, lava phases start short (around 4 seconds) and grow to as long as 14 seconds by the end.

<Tip>
  Use Marea for longer rounds (2:30 or 4 minutes) to feel the full escalation. A short round ends before the tide fully comes in, which wastes the best part of this difficulty.
</Tip>

**What to expect:** The first half of a Marea round feels almost like Fácil. Then, around the halfway point, everything accelerates. Players who conserve energy early will have an advantage when the pace becomes relentless.

---

## Traición

**Spanish name:** Traición

Traición ("Betrayal") is Normal-speed chaos with psychological warfare built in. Safe phases last 4–9 seconds and lava phases 6–12 seconds — but there are two extra mechanics:

- **Fake lava (35% chance):** The warning sound plays and the screen goes amber, but the lava never comes. The voice announces *'¡Falsa alarma!'* and play continues. Players who panic and jump early are exposed.
- **Sneak lava (30% chance):** Lava hits with no warning phase at all — the screen jumps straight to red. This bypasses the amber *'¡Prepárate!'* stage entirely.

<Warning>
  In Traición, disabling the warning phase in settings removes all sneak-lava surprise since every transition is already unwarned. Keep the warning phase enabled to get the full Traición experience.
</Warning>

**What to expect:** A constant state of suspicion. You will start second-guessing every warning, and the sneak phases will punish anyone who has relaxed too much.

---

## Caos

**Spanish name:** Caos

Caos ("Chaos") is the hardest pace. Safe phases last only 2.5–6 seconds — barely enough time to reposition — while lava phases run 8–15 seconds. There is no pattern, no mercy, and very little breathing room.

<Warning>
  Caos can be genuinely exhausting. Make sure your play space is safe for rapid movement and that all players are physically comfortable with fast, repeated scrambles onto furniture.
</Warning>

**What to expect:** Near-constant lava. Even experienced players lose lives quickly. Keep rounds short (45 seconds or 1:30) unless your group is specifically looking for a brutal endurance challenge.

---

## Recommendations

Choosing the right pace is the single biggest factor in how much fun your group has.

  <Card title="Families with kids" icon="children">
    Start with **Siesta** for the youngest players, then graduate to **Fácil** once everyone knows the rules.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Casual groups" icon="people-group">
    **Normal** or **Metrónomo** work well for mixed groups of adults playing together for the first time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Competitive players" icon="fire">
    **Volcán** and **Caos** reward athleticism and quick decision-making. Combine with Supervivencia or Cacería for high-stakes rounds.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Escalating tension" icon="wave-square">
    **Marea** is perfect when you want a round that builds to a climax — pair it with a 4-minute or Maratón round length.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Chaotic fun" icon="shuffle">
    **Traición** keeps everyone paranoid and laughing. Great for groups who think they have mastered the game.
  </Card>
