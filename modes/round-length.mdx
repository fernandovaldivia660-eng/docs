Round length determines how long each individual game lasts before a winner is declared. Shorter rounds keep things fast and give everyone frequent chances to reset; longer rounds build genuine tension and give objectives like Cacería or Ida y Vuelta enough time to develop. You set the round length in the game configuration before each session, and you can change it between rounds without restarting the app.

## Available round lengths

| Option | Spanish name | Duration | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relámpago | Relámpago | 45 seconds | Quick warm-ups, tiebreakers |
| Normal | Normal | 1 minute 30 seconds | First-time players, mixed groups |
| Larga | Larga | 2 minutes 30 seconds | Established groups wanting more play time |
| Aguante | Aguante | 4 minutes | Competitive sessions, Marea difficulty |
| Maratón | Maratón | 6 minutes | Dedicated endurance challenges |
| Sin límite | Sin límite | Unlimited | You decide when it ends |

<Tip>
  If your group is playing El Piso es Lava for the first time, start with **Normal (1:30)**. It is long enough to experience several lava phases and understand the flow, but short enough that nobody gets bored or overly tired before they have found their feet.
</Tip>

---

## Relámpago — Lightning (45 s)

**Spanish name:** Relámpago

At 45 seconds, a Relámpago round is over almost before it begins. There are only a handful of lava phases, which makes every single one feel critical. One mistake can cost you the whole round.

**When to use it:**
- As a warm-up at the start of a session to get everyone moving.
- As a sudden-death tiebreaker between two players with equal scores.
- With Caos or Volcán difficulty, where the pace is already relentless.

---

## Normal — 1:30

**Spanish name:** Normal

The default round length gives you enough lava phases to build a meaningful score without wearing anyone out. Most difficulty paces fit comfortably into 1:30, and it is the length the game is balanced around.

**When to use it:**
- Your group's go-to length for casual sessions.
- Any objective, any difficulty, any group size.

---

## Larga — Long (2:30)

**Spanish name:** Larga

Two and a half minutes adds a second wind to every round. Players who fall behind early have time to catch up, and Cacería rounds become genuinely tactical as the item list grows.

**When to use it:**
- Cacería, where more time means more items and a more decisive winner.
- Groups who feel that 1:30 rounds end just when things get interesting.

---

## Aguante — Endurance (4 min)

**Spanish name:** Aguante

Four minutes is a serious commitment. Lava phases accumulate and physical fatigue becomes a real factor, especially on harder difficulty paces. Aguante rounds suit groups who want their game sessions to feel like an event.

<Note>
  Aguante pairs especially well with **Marea** difficulty. The escalating pace of Marea takes around two minutes to reach its most intense point — a four-minute round gives you the full arc, from a relaxed opening to a frantic finish.
</Note>

**When to use it:**
- Marea difficulty for the full escalation experience.
- Competitive groups running a multi-round tournament.

---

## Maratón — Marathon (6 min)

**Spanish name:** Maratón

Six minutes of El Piso es Lava is a genuine test of stamina and focus. By the end of a Maratón round, even the most athletic players will be feeling it. Strategy matters more here — conserving energy, choosing stable furniture, and not burning out in the first two minutes.

<Warning>
  On Caos or Volcán difficulty, a six-minute Maratón round is extremely demanding. Make sure all players are physically up for it and that your play space is free of hazards before you commit to this length.
</Warning>

**When to use it:**
- A climactic final round at the end of a session.
- Small, competitive groups who specifically want an endurance challenge.

---

## Sin límite — Unlimited (∞)

**Spanish name:** Sin límite

In unlimited mode, the round has no timer. The game runs indefinitely until you manually tap **'Terminar ronda'** (End Round) to stop it. This is the most flexible option — you decide when enough is enough.

**How it works:**
- The **'¡Diez segundos!'** countdown announcement does not play in unlimited mode since there is no end time.
- Tap **'Terminar ronda'** at any moment to end the round immediately and calculate scores.
- The winner is determined the same way as timed rounds: most lives remaining, or most items collected in Cacería mode.

**When to use it:**
- Free-play sessions where you want to go until the group naturally winds down.
- Testing a new objective or difficulty setting without committing to a fixed length.
- When younger players are involved and you want to end on a positive note for everyone.

---

## The 10-second countdown

In all timed round lengths, when exactly 10 seconds remain on the clock, the Spanish voice announces **'¡Diez segundos!'**. This is your signal that the round is almost over — use those final seconds wisely, especially in Cacería, where one last item fetch could change the result.

---

## Determining the winner

When time expires (or you tap 'Terminar ronda' in unlimited mode), the winner is determined as follows:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Compare lives remaining">
    The player with the most lives at the end of the round wins. This applies to all objectives except Cacería.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cacería: compare items collected">
    In Cacería mode, lives are secondary. The player who fetched the most items wins the round, regardless of lives remaining.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resolve ties">
    If two or more players are tied on lives (or items in Cacería), the player with the higher score total breaks the tie.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Multi-round sessions

A single session of El Piso es Lava can include as many rounds as you like. Round wins accumulate across the session so you can crown an overall champion.

- After each round ends, the app shows that round's results and lets you start the next round immediately with the same settings, or adjust difficulty, objective, or round length before continuing.
- When your group is finished playing, tap **'Terminar partida'** (End Session) to see the full final standings — total wins, total lives lost, and items collected across all rounds.

<Info>
  The session standings screen is a great way to settle any disputes about who *really* won the night, especially after a long run of quick Relámpago rounds.
</Info>
